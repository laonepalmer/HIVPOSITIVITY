The answers you need

Common questions

Tap a question to read the answer. A healthcare provider can give advice that fits your health and circumstances.

Can I live a normal, healthy life with HIV?

Yes. HIV treatment (ARV) can control the virus and protect your immune system. Taking it consistently and keeping clinic appointments can help you live a long, healthy life.

What happens if I miss a dose?

Take the missed dose as soon as you remember, unless it is almost time for your next dose. Do not take a double dose. If this happens often, ask your clinic for advice and help finding a routine that works for you.

Are there side effects from ARV?

Some people have side effects, especially when starting a new medicine. They are often manageable. Do not stop treatment on your own—speak with your healthcare provider, who can help you manage symptoms or review your treatment.

Can I stop taking my medication if I feel better?

No. Feeling better often means your treatment is working. Stopping ARV can allow HIV to increase again and may make treatment harder in the future. Talk to your healthcare provider before making any change.

Can I have a partner if I am living with HIV?

Yes. People living with HIV can have loving, healthy relationships. When ARV keeps your viral load undetectable, HIV is not sexually transmitted (U=U). An HIV-negative partner can also ask a Botswana clinic about PrEP, a medicine that helps prevent HIV; Botswana’s HIV guidelines include PrEP for partners in HIV-discordant relationships. Condoms still help prevent other sexually transmitted infections and pregnancy.

Can HIV be passed from mother to child?

HIV can be passed during pregnancy, birth or breastfeeding, but this risk can be greatly reduced. Botswana provides prevention of mother-to-child transmission services. HIV testing in pregnancy, starting and staying on ARV, viral-load monitoring and the baby’s recommended care all matter. Speak with an antenatal or HIV clinic early for a personal pregnancy and infant-feeding plan.

Where can I get tested for HIV?

HIV testing is available through government health facilities and dedicated testing services in Botswana. Visit a clinic near you or see the support and resources page for starting points.
